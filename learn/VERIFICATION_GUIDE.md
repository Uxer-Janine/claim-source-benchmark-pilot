# Use the five verification principles

This guide helps beginners examine whether a source supports a claim, then apply the same reasoning in another field. It follows **Understand → Observe → Practice → Apply → Review**. You can read and copy the worksheet without installing software or creating an account.

The current learning focus is **A01 → A04 → A03**, selected from the original five Wikipedia cases. All original assessments are provisional. The [case index](../docs/INVESTIGATIONS.md) explains which cases are active and which are reserved for future expansion.

## Understand

A claim is a statement you can check. A citation identifies the source it relies on. Evidence is the part of that source relevant to the statement.

Checking that source is one task. Establishing whether the statement is true may require other sources. A source that leaves a detail unanswered does not prove the detail false.

### The five principles

1. **Identify the claim.** Copy the wording and note its context. Who or what does it describe, and under which conditions?
2. **Break it down.** Separate details that could have different answers. For example, an amount, who estimated it, and the number of clinics need separate checks.
3. **Examine the evidence.** Read the source around the relevant passage. Record the version and location. Check dates, places, measurements, and test conditions.
4. **Make a reasoned judgment.** Explain what each passage supports and what it leaves open. Distinguish conflicting evidence from missing evidence. If you cannot access enough material, explain that limit and leave the verdict unset.
5. **Document and review.** Keep the evidence location, reasoning, confidence, and uncertainty together. Ask another reader to save their own assessment before seeing yours. Preserve disagreements and any later review decision.

The [assessment guide](../annotation-guidelines.md#verdicts) gives the existing verdict definitions and the rules for combining individual judgments. It includes recorded case assessments.

Try [the written A04 exercise](README.md#a04--surrogacy-in-india) or [Evidence Desk](https://uxer-janine.github.io/claim-source-benchmark-pilot/lab/game/). ## Apply — A claim from your own field

Copy the [blank verification worksheet](VERIFICATION_WORKSHEET.md#blank-worksheet). Use it for an engineering report, academic paper, education study, public-policy document, or another statement with cited evidence.

The worksheet’s fictional solar-irrigation example shows how to ask for measurements, a comparable cost analysis, and evidence of reliability in the stated environment. It supplies no experiment, source, measurements, or verdict. A result under one set of conditions cannot establish that it holds everywhere.

## Review

Give another reader the claim, source, and worksheet without your judgment first. Record what they could access and any earlier exposure to other judgments. Compare the passages and reasons behind your conclusions.

Keep both assessments. Record disagreements about wording, evidence, or how the claim was split. For scored use in this pilot, the existing [independent-review requirements](../annotation-guidelines.md#independent-review-and-adjudication) still apply. A completed worksheet alone does not approve a reference for scoring.

## What carries over?

**Reusable:** separating details, finding passages, documenting reasoning, handling uncertainty, independent review, and keeping disagreements visible.

**Specific to this pilot:** the Wikipedia cases, original research records, schema, evaluator, historical AI responses, and Evidence Desk’s investigation. This guide does not establish that the software has been validated in other fields.
